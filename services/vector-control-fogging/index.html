Mosquitoes, flies and other disease-carrying insects are known as vectors because they can spread illnesses such as dengue, malaria and chikungunya. What Is Vector Control and Why Larviciding Plus Fogging Works Best

Vector control means reducing insects and other pests that carry and spread disease. Good vector control works in three ways:

  1. Reduce adult insects with suitable fogging and spraying.
  2. Stop breeding by treating water and breeding sites with larvicide where appropriate.
  3. Prevent return through source reduction and practical awareness.

Fogging alone provides short-term relief because it targets adult mosquitoes present during treatment. More complete control also addresses breeding sites, which is why we include site assessment and larvicide recommendations in the plan.

Protecting Communities from Dengue, Malaria & Chikungunya Vectors

  • Dengue: spread by Aedes mosquitoes, which can breed in clean stored water.
  • Malaria: spread by Anopheles mosquitoes.
  • Chikungunya: spread by Aedes mosquitoes.
  • Filariasis: spread by Culex mosquitoes.
  • Food-borne illnesses: houseflies can transfer contamination to food.

Mosquito activity is often highest during and after the monsoon, so planning ahead can help reduce breeding and adult populations.

Key Signs Your Society, Campus or Venue Needs Mosquito Treatment

  • Many mosquitoes inside, especially in the morning and evening
  • Standing water in coolers, tanks, tyres, drains or pits
  • Dengue or malaria cases in your area or society
  • Flies around kitchens, garbage areas, dairies or food premises
  • Open drains, waterlogging or nearby ponds, fields or construction sites
  • Daytime mosquito bites, which may indicate Aedes activity

Practical Stagnant-Water & Screening Steps to Reduce Mosquito Breeding

  • Empty and dry coolers, flower pots, tyres and containers every week.
  • Cover water tanks and drums tightly.
  • Fix leaking taps and remove waterlogging.
  • Keep drains clean and free-flowing.
  • Use mosquito nets and window screens.
  • Keep garbage covered and dispose of it daily.
  • Wear full-sleeve clothes in the evening during peak season.

Integrating Source Reduction into Daily Facility Maintenance Routines

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) for flying pests combines inspection, breeding-source reduction, physical barriers and carefully selected treatments where needed. Before repeating fogging, review whether water is collecting in a cooler, container, drain or low point, and whether that source can be removed or managed by the site. Adult-insect treatments and source management address different parts of the problem; a plan should state which actions belong to the service team and which depend on the property operator.

Sanitation and exclusion steps can reduce opportunities for flies and mosquitoes to enter or breed around occupied buildings. Keep waste containers covered, maintain screens and door closers where fitted, and report damaged mesh or gaps that leave an opening. Remove standing water where practical and arrange maintenance for blocked drains or leaks. These measures are especially useful around shared entrances, kitchens, waste rooms and loading areas, but the site should choose actions that fit its operations and avoid unsafe access to tanks or drains.

For a school, housing society, food business or facility with multiple blocks, a simple pest-control log can make follow-up clearer. Record the observation location, date, weather or site condition if useful, the breeding source reported, work completed and any maintenance action still open. A log is an operational aid, not proof that every risk has been eliminated. Staff should know whom to notify about water pooling or a damaged screen, and should not apply larvicide or other products unless this is part of their authorized role.

At a food-handling site, coordinate pest prevention with the operator’s existing cleaning and hygiene procedures; do not fog or apply products around exposed food. Should fogging replace removal of standing water?

No. Fogging targets flying adults present during treatment; source reduction addresses breeding conditions and should be considered separately.

What can a facility record between visits?

Note sightings, the specific location, standing-water concerns, screen or drain issues and any maintenance action awaiting completion.

Frequently Asked Questions About Mosquito Fogging & Vector Control

What is the difference between fogging and vector control?

Fogging kills adult flying insects present during treatment. Vector control is a broader approach that also stops breeding with larvicide, treats drains and water sources, and includes prevention advice.

How often should fogging be done?

During monsoon and peak mosquito season, fogging may be repeated at regular intervals, such as weekly or every 15 days, depending on the site and inspection findings.

Is fogging safe for children and pets?

When done by trained professionals with approved solutions, follow the safety steps provided by our technician. We explain how long people and pets should stay out of the treated area and what to cover beforehand.

What is the difference between thermal fogging and ULV fogging?

Thermal fogging uses heat to create a dense fog for suitable outdoor areas. ULV cold fogging uses tiny droplets without heat and suits indoor or sensitive areas where heat fogging is not appropriate.

Does fogging stop dengue?

Fogging helps reduce adult mosquitoes, but dengue control also depends on removing and treating Aedes breeding sites, including stored clean water. That is why we combine appropriate fogging with larvicide and source-reduction advice.

Why do mosquitoes come back soon after fogging?

New mosquitoes can emerge from untreated breeding spots such as stagnant water. Larvicide treatment and suitable follow-up help address those sources.

Can you do fogging for a society, school or hospital?

Yes. We handle large campuses and can discuss regular schedules and AMC plans for societies, schools, hospitals and businesses.

Can I book fogging before a wedding or an event?

Yes. Event and venue fogging can be scheduled, ideally a day before the event. Contact us to discuss timing and site requirements.

How much does fogging cost in Rajasthan?

The price depends on the area, treatment method, number of visits and mosquito activity. We provide a clear quote after a site visit.

Do you offer an AMC for vector control?

Yes. Seasonal and yearly plans are available for societies, institutions, hotels, factories and homes.
