Why Subterranean Termites Are the Most Destructive Threat to Woodwork & Buildings

Termites live in large colonies and feed on wood, paper, cardboard and other cellulose materials. They usually work out of sight. Moisture around plumbing, leaks, coolers and gardens can create conditions that help them reach a building.

Left untreated, termites can weaken door and window frames, damage floors and furniture, ruin stored books and documents, and lead to repair bills far higher than the cost of early treatment.

Early Signs of Termites: Mud Tubes, Hollow Wood, Frass & Swarming Alates

  • Mud tubes along walls, skirting or foundations
  • A hollow sound when you tap on wood
  • Bubbling or peeling paint and damp-looking patches
  • Discarded wings near windows and doors after rain
  • Wood that crumbles or feels soft
  • Small piles of droppings that resemble fine sawdust or grains

If you notice these signs, arrange an inspection early to help limit damage and treatment costs.

Practical Moisture Control & Timber Care Tips to Prevent Termites

  • Repair leaking pipes, taps and water coolers promptly.
  • Keep wood, cardboard and old papers off floors and away from damp walls.
  • Avoid storing firewood or wooden scrap against the building.
  • Improve drainage around the foundation and garden.
  • Arrange a yearly inspection, especially if the property had termites before.

Frequently Asked Questions About Termite Treatment & Warranty

How do I know if I have termites?

Look for mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood, damp-looking walls and discarded wings. A professional inspection confirms the infestation and how far it has spread.

What is the difference between pre-construction and post-construction termite treatment?

Pre-construction treatment is done while a building is being built by treating the soil and foundation. Post-construction treatment is done in an existing building by drilling and injecting termiticide along floors and walls.

How long does the treatment take?

Most homes are treated in a few hours to one day, depending on the property size and the extent of the infestation.

Is termite treatment safe for children and pets?

Comparing Post-Construction Drilling, Soil Treatment & Termite Piping

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) begins with evidence from the property rather than selecting one treatment for every building. An inspection can distinguish visible termite signs from other wood damage, note moisture or access concerns, and help define which parts of a home or business need attention. The technician should explain the recommended scope, preparation and limitations before work begins. Fixing leaks and keeping stored timber away from damp surfaces complement treatment, but do not replace an assessment where activity is suspected.

For an existing building, drill-and-inject treatment may be considered where the inspection and construction make it suitable. The location of holes, areas included and any repair or finishing needed should be discussed before application. A baiting approach is a different option that may be considered in some circumstances; it is not automatically interchangeable with a soil or injection treatment, and availability and suitability must be confirmed for the property. Ask the provider to explain why the proposed method fits the signs and access conditions found.

Monitoring after service means watching for new evidence and reporting it, not assuming that every mark indicates treatment failure. Keep the written scope and warranty details with the property records, note where fresh mud tubes or damaged wood appear, and contact the team with the location and date. Follow-up visits, if offered, should state what is inspected and whether any additional work is quoted separately. Avoid drilling, covering or applying products to a treated area without first checking the service instructions.

Owners planning a new build can review pre-construction treatment stages; projects considering recharge access can compare the termite piping system. For wood damage that may have another cause, see wood-borer identification and treatment before deciding on a method.

Is baiting the same as drill-and-inject treatment?

No. They are different approaches, and the suitable method depends on inspection findings, building access and what the provider offers for the site.

What should I note when reporting new termite signs?

Share the date and exact location, describe what you observed, and avoid disturbing the area until the service team advises you.
