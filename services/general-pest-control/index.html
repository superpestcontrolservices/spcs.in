What Is General Pest Control (GPC) and What Does It Cover?

General Pest Control is a combined treatment for common household and commercial pests in one visit. Instead of treating each pest separately, technicians focus on the areas where pests hide, breed and travel, such as kitchens, bathrooms, drains, cupboards, corners and gaps.

Bed bugs, termites and rodents need dedicated treatments, which we offer separately.

Household Pests Covered: Cockroaches, Ants, Spiders, Silverfish & Lizards

We treat common crawling and flying pests around homes and workplaces. The exact method depends on the pest, property and inspection.

  • Cockroaches: Commonly found in kitchens, drains and cupboards; they can spread germs and contaminate food.
  • Ants: Attracted to food and sweets, they can invade kitchens and storerooms.
  • Flies: Can carry bacteria from dirty areas to food and preparation surfaces.
  • Mosquitoes: Can spread diseases such as dengue and malaria.
  • Spiders: Webs and nests can build up in corners, ceilings and storage areas.
  • Silverfish: May damage books, papers and clothes in damp places.
  • Lizards: We can provide control and deterrence advice for kitchens and bedrooms.
  • Other crawling and flying insects found around your property.

Bed bugs, termites and rodents need special treatment, so we handle them through dedicated services.

Top Signs Your Home or Workplace Needs General Pest Control

  • Cockroaches appear at night or when you switch on the kitchen light.
  • Ant trails cross floors, walls or counters.
  • Flies and mosquitoes keep returning despite regular cleaning.
  • Droppings, egg cases or unusual smells appear around kitchens or drains.
  • Cobwebs build up quickly in corners.
  • You are opening a home, restaurant or office and want it pest-free from day one.

Combining Gel Baiting, Dusting & Targeted Barrier Sprays Safely

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) brings inspection findings, site conditions and suitable control methods into one plan rather than treating every report with the same product. The pest, affected area, occupants and operating routine help determine which measures are relevant. The plan should explain what action is being considered, why it fits the findings and what the property team needs to do. IPM does not mean that every method is used at every visit, and it should not be presented as a guarantee that pests will never return.

Mechanical and physical controls may include barriers, screens, door sweeps or suitable traps, depending on the pest and the property. These measures can limit access or help monitor activity, but their placement and maintenance matter. A device should not block an exit, interfere with business operations or be moved without guidance when its position is part of an agreed plan. Ask which person is responsible for checking or maintaining each measure.

Biological control is a specialized option, not a universal substitute for other pest-management work. It should only be considered when a suitable, lawful and site-appropriate approach exists for the identified pest and setting. The provider should explain what is meant by the proposed method, what it can and cannot address, and whether it is available for the property. The word “natural” alone does not establish that a method is safe, effective or appropriate.

Monitoring can help the property team see whether activity changes after the agreed actions and decide if the plan needs review. The provider and customer should confirm what is checked, who receives the findings and whether a follow-up visit is included in the service terms. Keep observations distinct from assumptions and use the results to guide next steps, not as proof that an uninspected area is pest-free. Questions about integrated pest management

Does IPM mean chemical treatment is never used?

No. The methods depend on the findings and the agreed plan; suitable options should be explained before work begins.

Can a trap or barrier replace an inspection?

No. Physical measures may support a plan, but the pest and site conditions still need appropriate assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions About General Pest Control (GPC)

What is included in general pest control?

GPC covers common pests such as cockroaches, ants, flies, mosquitoes, spiders and silverfish. Termites, bed bugs and rodents need separate specialist treatments.

Is general pest control safe for my family and pets?

We use approved products and follow safety steps. Our technician will explain which areas to avoid and for how long after treatment.

How long does the treatment take?

Most homes take about 1 to 3 hours, depending on the property size and infestation level.

How often should I get pest control done?

For homes, once or twice a year is usually enough. Restaurants, offices and commercial places may benefit from monthly or quarterly service.

Do I need to leave the house during treatment?

In most cases you need to stay out for a short time after treatment. Your technician will tell you exactly how long.

How much does general pest control cost in Rajasthan?

The price depends on the property size, pests involved and infestation level. We provide a clear quote after inspection.

Can I get a regular service plan?

Yes. Monthly, quarterly and yearly pest control plans are available for homes and businesses.

What is a pest control AMC?

An Annual Maintenance Contract is a yearly plan with regular pest control visits at a fixed cost, helping protect your property throughout the year.

Is an AMC worth it for a home or business?

An AMC can suit restaurants, offices and commercial places where pests return often and hygiene matters. It can also suit homes that want scheduled protection.
