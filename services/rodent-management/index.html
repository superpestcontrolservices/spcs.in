Why Rodent Infestations Pose Severe Structural, Fire & Health Risks

Rodents are more than a nuisance. They can cause health, property and business problems.

  • Health risks: germs can spread through droppings, urine and contaminated food.
  • Fire hazards: gnawed electrical wires can cause short circuits.
  • Property damage: rodents chew furniture, clothes, books, pipes and insulation.
  • Stock and food loss: they eat and spoil grain, packaged food and stored goods.
  • Business risk: rodent activity can affect hygiene audits and reputation.

Key Signs of Rats and Mice: Droppings, Gnaw Marks, Burrows & Runway Smears

  • Droppings in kitchens, cupboards, storerooms or along walls
  • Gnaw marks on wood, wires, packaging or food
  • Scratching or running sounds in ceilings, walls or at night
  • Greasy rub marks along walls and skirting
  • A strong, musty smell
  • Burrows near foundations, drains or gardens
  • Torn bags or damaged stored goods

If you notice these signs, arrange an inspection early. Rodents multiply quickly, so early action can help limit damage.

Audit-Ready Rodent Bait Maps & Trend Logs for Food and Industrial Sites

Where a warehouse, kitchen or food-production area reports rodent signs, the food business should assess the location and affected materials through its own documented controls. Pest-service information can support an operator’s HACCP documentation by describing the area assessed and the agreed pest-control response. It cannot determine whether a product is safe or replace the business’s responsibility to make that decision.

For an FSSAI-related pest-control programme, identify who receives reports, how the operator evaluates a concern and who can authorize corrective action. The service provider can explain its own scope and help organize relevant information, but it does not issue licences or approve a food-safety system. The competent authority makes licensing decisions; formal certification is provided by an accredited certification body.

Food-site records should distinguish an observed sign from an unverified conclusion. The operator may capture the date, affected zone, product or packaging information available, action referred for review and the person responsible for follow-up. Do not move or dispose of potentially affected stock solely on a pest report; follow the business’s own handling and escalation procedure and obtain appropriate guidance.

For an audit or internal review, gather the current rodent-service scope and the relevant operator-controlled documents, then identify questions that need verification. Staff should know how to report an observation and whom to contact, without moving control devices or handling bait unless specifically authorized. Brief the team using the site’s own reporting route, and make clear that workers should not decide product impact or take actions outside their assigned role. This awareness should complement the food business’s approved training and escalation procedures. Questions for food-site managers

Does a rodent sighting automatically mean stock is unsafe?

No. The responsible food-safety lead must assess the specific product and circumstances under the site’s procedures.

Can a pest provider approve a food-business corrective action?

No. The operator’s designated personnel retain responsibility for its food-safety decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions About Rat & Rodent Control

How do I know if I have rats or mice?

Look for droppings, gnaw marks, scratching sounds, greasy rub marks and a musty smell. An inspection confirms the rodent type and the extent of the problem.

What is the best way to get rid of rats?

Rodent control works best when control methods are combined with sealing entry points and reducing access to food. Baiting alone may fail if rodents can still enter and find food.

Is rodent control safe for children and pets?

Our technicians select and place control methods with children and pets in mind, using secure bait stations where appropriate, and explain the precautions to follow.

How long does it take to remove rodents?

Many infestations improve within a few days, but full control can take a few weeks depending on infestation size and the number of entry points.

Why do rats keep coming back?

Rodents return when food, water, shelter and open entry points remain available. Proofing and sanitation advice are important parts of prevention.

Will you also seal the entry points?

Our technicians inspect the property, find entry points such as gaps around pipes, doors, vents and drains, and tell you exactly what needs to be sealed. The sealing work itself can be done by your mason or maintenance team, and our advice helps them block every route so rodents cannot return.

How much does rodent control cost in Rajasthan?

The price depends on property size, rodent type and infestation level. We provide a clear quote after inspection.

Do you offer an AMC for rodent control?

Yes. AMC plans provide regular visits and monitoring for businesses such as restaurants, godowns, offices and factories.
