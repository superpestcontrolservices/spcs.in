Using Insect Growth Regulators (IGRs) in Commercial Kitchen IPM

Gel baiting is already one of the targeted options described on this page. In some situations, a technician may also discuss an insect growth regulator (IGR) as part of a site-specific cockroach plan. An IGR is not an instant substitute for addressing visible adult activity, cleaning conditions or access routes. Its suitability depends on the pest, the premises, product directions and the other methods included in the agreed scope. Ask what role it is intended to play rather than assuming every kitchen needs the same combination.

For a restaurant or commercial kitchen, pest-control decisions may need to sit alongside the operator’s HACCP implementation and documentation. Support can clarify how the pest service relates to the food business’s own controls and who is responsible for reviewing proposed actions. It does not replace the operator’s hazard analysis, food-safety procedures or approval decisions. A home visit and a commercial-kitchen plan should not be treated as equivalent simply because both report cockroach activity.

Where relevant, the food business can ask whether the proposed pest-control programme supports its FSSAI-related process and how the service scope will be described. Super Pest Control Services may help organize supporting information, but it does not issue an FSSAI licence or a HACCP certificate. Licensing decisions rest with the competent authority, and formal certification is handled by an accredited certification body. Verify current requirements from official sources for the business concerned.

Before a food-safety review, a manager can prepare questions about treatment areas, access restrictions and the responsibilities assigned to the pest provider and kitchen team. A discussion can help identify items that need clarification; it cannot promise an audit result or confirm compliance. Questions for food-service managers

Does using an IGR by itself resolve every cockroach concern?

No. The technician should explain its intended role within the assessed, site-specific plan.

Can a pest-control provider certify our HACCP system?

No. The provider can discuss pest-related documentation, but formal certification is issued by an accredited certification body.
