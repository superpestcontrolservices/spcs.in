How an Integrated Fly Management System Protects Your Property

A fly management system is a planned, long-term approach to controlling flies, not just a one-time spray. It combines four things:

  • Finding and removing breeding sources such as waste, drains and wet organic matter
  • Keeping flies out with screens, air curtains and proofing
  • Trapping and killing adult flies with insect light traps and other devices
  • Treating surfaces and breeding places with approved products

Spraying alone gives only short-term relief, because new flies keep arriving from breeding sites and open doors. A system that deals with the cause is what delivers lasting results.

Why it matters

Flies are more than an annoyance. They land on garbage, drains and waste, then land on your food, carrying germs with them.

For restaurants, food factories, dairies and hospitals, flies can mean failed hygiene audits, customer complaints and lost business.

Common Fly Species Treated: Houseflies, Drain Flies & Fruit Flies

  • Houseflies: found around garbage, kitchens, food stalls and drains, and spread germs to food
  • Fruit flies: found around fruit, vegetable stores, bars and kitchens
  • Drain flies (moth flies): found in drains, sewer points and damp bathrooms
  • Blowflies (bottle flies): found around meat shops, waste and dead matter
  • Flesh flies: found around waste and animal areas
  • Cluster and other flies: found in some buildings in season
  • Small flies around dairies, farms and poultry units

Health, Contamination & FSSAI Audit Risks Caused by Fly Infestations

  • Flies spread germs that cause stomach infections and food-borne illness
  • Contaminated food hurts customer trust and your brand
  • Flies can cause failed hygiene inspections and audits
  • Complaints, bad reviews and loss of customers
  • Food spoilage and product losses
  • Stress and discomfort for staff and residents

Signs Your Kitchen, Facility or Venue Needs Professional Fly Control

  • Many flies around the kitchen, dining area, counter or entrance
  • Flies gathering around garbage bins, drains and waste areas
  • Tiny flies rising from drains or wet floors
  • Maggots in bins, drains or waste
  • Flies landing on food even when you keep the place clean
  • Fly complaints from customers, staff or auditors
  • A dairy, farm, market or neighbouring area with heavy fly activity

Sanitation, Air-Curtain & Waste-Management Steps to Prevent Flies

  • Keep garbage in covered bins and empty them daily
  • Clean spills, food waste and wet areas right away
  • Keep drains clean and flowing, and cover open drains
  • Use fine mesh screens on windows and doors, and keep doors closed
  • Use air curtains at entrances in kitchens and food areas
  • Store food in sealed containers
  • Wash bins and waste areas regularly with a proper cleaner
  • Remove rotting fruit, vegetables and organic waste quickly
  • Manage manure, animal waste and wet fodder properly on farms
  • Place insect light traps away from food preparation tables, in line with our technician's advice

Aligning Fly Trap Catch Logs with Commercial Food-Safety Procedures

Restaurants, dairies and food factories may need to explain how fly activity is addressed within their HACCP documentation. A site discussion can map the reported area to the operator’s own controls and establish who reviews the proposed pest-service work. The provider can describe its scope and observations, but the food business retains responsibility for its hazard analysis, product decisions and procedure approvals.

Where FSSAI requirements are relevant, the operator should verify current official guidance for its activity and ask how the pest-control programme can be documented as one part of its food-safety arrangements. A service visit or control device does not by itself prove compliance. Super Pest Control Services does not issue FSSAI licences or certificates; licensing decisions belong to the authority, and formal HACCP certification is provided by an accredited certification body.

Hygiene and sanitation SOPs should assign clear responsibilities for waste handling, cleaning, drain or equipment access and reporting signs of fly activity. The pest-control provider can discuss how the service fits around these routines, while the operator approves its procedures and determines how they are used. Avoid changing a cleaning or food-handling process without the site’s authorization.

Staff food-safety training can help workers identify whom to notify when they see activity or a condition needing review. It should be specific to the site’s roles and complement, rather than replace, the operator’s approved training. Before an audit, managers may gather the current service scope and prepare questions for the responsible food-safety lead; no audit result should be promised. Questions for food businesses

Does a fly-control service certify a food business?

No. The provider can describe its pest work, but regulatory and certification decisions are made by the appropriate bodies.

Can staff change cleaning procedures after a service visit?

Any procedure change should be reviewed and approved by the food business’s responsible person.

Frequently Asked Questions About Fly Control & UV Light Traps

What is a fly management system?

It is a long-term plan that finds and treats breeding sources, keeps flies out, traps adult flies and monitors results, instead of just spraying when flies appear.

Why do flies keep coming back even after spraying?

Because new flies come from breeding places like drains, waste, manure and wet organic matter. To stop the problem at its root, the breeding source must be treated, not just the visible flies.

Are insect light traps effective for restaurants?

Yes, when they are placed correctly. They help reduce adult fly numbers and support hygiene without placing sticky traps near food prep surfaces.

Can you handle fly problems in hospitals and food units?

Yes. We plan service around hygiene compliance and can advise on high-risk areas such as waste rooms, kitchens, drains and entry points.
